What is the Paris Climate Agreement?

The Paris Climate Agreement is an international agreement created to help tackle climate change. It was adopted on 12 December 2015 at the United Nations Climate Change Conference in Paris, France. Almost every country in the world agreed to work together to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and limit the effects of global warming.

The main goal of the agreement is to keep the increase in global temperatures well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels, while aiming to limit the rise to 1.5°C. Scientists believe that staying closer to 1.5°C could reduce the risks of extreme weather events, rising sea levels, and damage to ecosystems.

Countries that joined the agreement create their own climate plans, known as Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs). These plans explain how each country will reduce emissions and adapt to the impacts of climate change. Countries are expected to update their plans regularly and make stronger commitments over time.

The Paris Agreement also encourages countries to invest in renewable energy, protect forests, improve energy efficiency, and support communities affected by climate change. Developed countries are encouraged to provide financial support and technology to help developing nations take climate action.

Climate change affects everyone, and the Paris Agreement shows the importance of global cooperation. By reducing pollution and making more sustainable choices, countries, businesses, and individuals can help create a cleaner and safer future for the planet.
